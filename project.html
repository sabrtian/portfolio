Overview

What we made, and why it matters.

Sensing Garden is an AI-enabled hardware product designed to passively monitor urban biodiversity — detecting insects, birds, and ambient conditions in real time. It needed to work outdoors, in all climates, without requiring expert installation.

My role spanned the full design arc: from the physical enclosure (balancing airflow mechanics with environmental durability) to the data visualization layer that made sensor readings legible to researchers and city planners.

Caption: Early prototype of the sensing enclosure, tested in Amsterdam, 2024.

The Challenge

Designing for the physical and digital simultaneously.

Most sensing devices optimize for either function (utilitarian, ugly) or aesthetics (designed for humans, not environments). We needed both.

01

Survive the field

Weatherproofed across humidity, rain, and temperature variance in 4 climate zones.

02

Allow passive airflow for acoustic sensing

Enclosure geometry had to permit sound entry for insect/bird detection without water ingress.

03

Enable non-expert installation

Research partners at 3 global workshops needed to deploy the sensor independently.

04

Render data legibly at scale

GIS-based visualizations had to work for expert researchers and 10k-person public events.

Left: airflow geometry studies. Right: weatherproofing stress tests.

Outcomes & Impact

What shipped, and what it did.

Two product generations deployed across Amsterdam, London, and two partner cities. GIS visualization system shown at a global biodiversity event with 10k+ attendees.

01

35% faster prototyping enabled self-serve deployment by 3 global research teams.

02

GIS visualizations adapted for both expert review and 10k-person public exhibition — zero redesign between contexts.

03

Workshop documentation designed for non-engineers eliminated direct support dependency.

04

Physical-digital touchpoint mapping across 2 product generations improved handoff between hardware and software teams.
